# 21. Bot players, run by the host

Status: Accepted (2026-11-10). Amends 0008 (the `Bot` export), 0012 and 0015 (seating, lobby
`players`), 0016 (seat book) and 0017 (waiting room, roster, selectors).

## Context

Players want to play alone, offline, or fill seats nobody took. Only formulas and control messages
travel, and every peer computes outcomes in WASM (ADR 2). The host sequences and relays everything
(ADR 12), keeps the log for replays (ADR 16) and owns the turn clock (ADR 15). The ten starter
templates (ADR 17, section 8) are formulas with sliders that parse and are finite at the shooter
over their whole slider range.

## Decision

- **Who runs bots.** The host adds bots to seats in the waiting room, at a level: Easy, Medium or
  Hard. The host's page runs every bot. A bot's shot is an ordinary `shot {turn, exprs, cw}` that
  the host relays on the bot's turn, so other peers, the log and the replay see only formulas.
  There is no new message type.
- **Seat ids.** A bot's entry in `start.peers` is `bot:<level>:<n>`, with `level` in
  `easy|medium|hard` and `n` the lowest free number from 1 to 7. Trystero peer ids are 20
  alphanumeric characters, so they cannot contain `:`. `parseGameMsg` drops a `start` with an
  entry that starts with `bot:` but does not match `^bot:(easy|medium|hard):[1-7]$`. Every peer
  derives the name from the id, "Bot 1, hard". `start` and the lobby listing gain no field.
- **Trust boundary.** Only the host acts for a bot seat. `seatBook` gives bot seats no token and no
  holder: `seatOf` never returns a bot seat (a peer announcing itself as `bot:…` gets nothing),
  `rejoin` cannot claim one, `absent` never lists one, and `relay(start)` sends no seat token to
  one. The host ignores a joining peer whose id starts with `bot:`, so it is never seated as a
  human. The host's bot driver passes the same check as a remote shot request (the seat of the
  current team, the current `turn`) before it relays.
- **Seating and lobby.** Bots keep their seats: `start.peers` is the host, then connected humans
  in join order up to `seats − 1 − bots`, then the bots. The listing's `players` counts the host,
  the connected humans and the bots, so a game whose other seats are all bots is listed as Full.
  Start needs 2 players, bots included. A guest's waiting room lists humans only, since nothing
  is sent before `start`; the roster shows the bots once the game starts.
- **Search in Rust** (`src/bot.rs`), exported like `Game` (ADR 8). `new Bot(game, level,
  starters)` copies the game and `think(budget): BotShot | undefined` simulates up to `budget`
  more candidates, returning `{exprs, cw}` once all of the level's candidates are done. `Level`
  is `Easy | Medium | Hard`, and `Starter` is the part of a starter the bot reads. `Game` exports
  nothing new: the bot reads the arena and the frame through crate-internal accessors and fires
  with `fire`/`fire_param` on its copy. In Rust a candidate costs no copy of the shot's points or
  the arena across the WASM boundary.
- **Randomness and determinism.** The bot only picks the text the host sends, as a human does by
  typing. It owns its own `SmallRng`, seeded from the operating system (`rand::make_rng`, through
  `getrandom` and `crypto.getRandomValues` in the browser), never from the game seed, and `Game`
  never holds it. The engine keeps no generator after `Game::new` (the seeded `SmallRng` is local
  to `init`), and the search fires only on the bot's copy, so nothing the bot does can reach the
  live game. A seeded constructor exists for the Rust tests only (`#[cfg(test)]`, not exported).
  `new Bot` borrows the game and keeps a deep copy, so the types keep the search off the live
  game. A Rust test guards it: after a full search the game's arena (alive flags, craters, each
  team's current player) and current team equal an untouched copy's, and the next real shot has
  the same outcome with or without the search. A test across the WASM boundary is left out:
  `npm test` runs before the WASM is built.
- **Templates.** The bot uses the starters for the game's field, not the host's saved formulas. The
  host passes it `js/starters.json`, checked with `parseItem` like a saved list, and tsify
  deserializes it (`from_wasm_abi`); the Rust tests read the same file with `include_str!` and
  `serde_json`, a dev-dependency. So the client and the bot share one list, and the WASM carries
  no JSON parser. On the disc in the player frame, the
  parametric starters' radial formula is negated (`-(-t)`), because `r = −t` leaves the disc at
  once in that frame (ADR 17, section 8). That raises Hard's first-shot hit rate there from 58%
  to 73% (calibration below). XY in both frames and the disc in the map frame use
  the starters unchanged. On the disc, every candidate also picks a direction (`cw`); XY sends
  `cw: false`. Slider values are continuous in `[min, max]`, not snapped to the step. They are
  written as `formatValue(v, 6)` in `js/library.ts` writes them, which is the text `substitute`
  gives for a slider whose step has 6 decimals (`toFixed`'s rounding of an exact tie away from
  zero included). A Rust test checks every starter at its min, its max and a random value: the
  text parses with the engine's parser and is at most 256 characters. Another compares values and
  formulas with texts produced by `library.ts`.
- **Search and score.** Score = 100 × enemy kills − 150 × own-team kills − miss. The miss is the
  smallest distance from the shot's points to a living enemy that survives, minus the player
  radius, floored at 0; points are at most 0.05 apart (ADR 14). Since a miss is under 45 on both
  fields, a shot that also kills a teammate always scores below a clean miss. The first half of
  the candidates sample template, values and direction uniformly. The best 3 distinct (template,
  direction) pairs then share the second half, round-robin. Each try is a Gaussian step per
  slider, σ from 0.15 down to 0.003 of the slider's span (geometric), and it is kept if it scores
  higher. A template without sliders is not refined.
- **Aim noise.** The chosen shot's slider values get Gaussian noise, σ = noise × (max − min),
  clamped to the limits, where every starter fires (tested), so the noisy shot is sent as it is.
  This is how a lower level misses a target it found.

  | Level | Candidates | Noise | First-shot hit rate: XY map / XY player / disc map / disc player | Search (Node, desktop) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Easy | 20 | 0.03 | 1% / 6% / 38% / 16% | 3 to 8 ms |
  | Medium | 100 | 0.004 | 33% / 36% / 86% / 53% | 21 to 37 ms |
  | Hard | 400 | 0.0003 | 65% / 65% / 93% / 73% | 77 to 155 ms |

  The rates were measured on 60 seeds with the seeded calibration in `src/bot.rs`
  (`cargo test --release calibrate -- --ignored --nocapture`): the client's arenas, XY with 2
  teams, the disc with 4, 3 players each. The search times are the mean per field and frame over
  20 seeds of the release WASM in Node, without the yields; the worst single search took 227 ms
  (Hard, disc). The values are starting points and live in one table, `Level::search`.
- **Turn budget.** The host calls `think(1)` for 10 ms, then yields to the event loop, so the page
  stays responsive; a single candidate took at most 1.4 ms in Node on a desktop. It yields with a
  `MessageChannel` message, not a timer: a hidden tab throttles timers to one a second (one a
  minute after 5 minutes), which would stretch a Hard search past the 60 s clock. The host relays the shot once the search is done and at least
  1.5 s have passed since the turn started, and only while the game and the turn are still the
  ones the bot searched. There is no Web Worker, which would need a second WASM instance and a
  copy of the game state. The host's 60 s clock still runs on a bot's turn and catches a bot that
  never fires.
- **Reconnection and leaving.** Bot shots are in the host's log, so a replay rebuilds them
  (ADR 16). Bots never quit and are never shown as disconnected. When the host leaves, the game
  ends as before, bots included.
- **Solo and offline.** Solo has no separate mode: host a game, add bots to the other seats, then
  Start. The game room is the usual Trystero room. With no peers nothing is sent, and offline its
  relay sockets just retry. The listing shows the game as Full until Start withdraws it. e2e S
  plays a whole game against a Hard bot with the network cut once the page has loaded, without
  page errors, so no local room without Trystero is built.
- **UI.** The waiting room, for the host only, has a "Bot level" select `#bot-level` (Easy,
  Medium (the default), Hard) and **Add bot** `#add-bot` (secondary). Add bot is disabled once bots
  fill `seats − 1`. Each bot is one `li` in `#room-players`, "Bot 1, hard", with a **Remove**
  button (`aria-label="Remove Bot 1"`). With more humans than seats, the host's line reads "the
  first `seats − bots` to join play". The roster shows the bot's name where a human's short id
  goes. The board does not change, and the status bar reads "Team 2 is aiming".

## Consequences

- No wire, replay or `Game` change. The new protocol rules are the bot id format and the seat
  book's handling of bot seats; the engine gains the `Bot`, `Level` and `Starter` exports.
- Solo play works offline once the page is loaded (e2e S).
- Bot strength follows the starters: changing a starter changes the bots. The hit rates were
  measured on first shots. Later turns have fewer targets and more craters.
- The host's CPU pays for the search. Phones may be several times slower, and the 10 ms slices
  keep the page responsive meanwhile. The 1.5 s wait is a timer, so a hidden host tab may fire a
  bot's shot later than that.
- A modified client can still forge any peer id (ADR 12). Bot seats are safe because the seat book
  never binds them to a peer.
- Tests: `tests/net.test.mjs` (bot ids, seat book), the unit tests in `src/bot.rs` (substitution,
  the live game left untouched, `think` in parts, a seeded hit rate), and e2e S (solo against a Hard
  bot, offline, to the end) and T (an online disc game with a bot seat and a guest reload). R was
  already taken by the player-frame direction scenario.
